GBV Call Center

AI Voice Agent in Swahili — Expanding lifeline access for survivors of gender-based violence

Gender-based violence is one of the most prevalent human rights violations in the world.

Violence against women and girls knows no social, economic, or national boundaries. In Tanzania, the scale demands urgent, scalable response.

1 in 3
Women worldwide will experience physical or sexual abuse in her lifetime
39.5%
Of married women aged 15–49 in Tanzania have experienced intimate partner violence
30%
Of girls experience sexual violence before the age of 18

UNFPA has already proven that helplines save lives.

UNFPA-supported interventions in Tanzania — including toll-free helplines, child protection committees, and safe spaces — have enabled children and communities to report abuse, prevent child marriage, and access critical services.

UN News: "Tanzania helpline calls time on child marriage and abuse" — documenting how UNFPA's hotline has become a lifeline for young girls across rural Tanzania. Read the full story →

Tanzania has a National Child Helpline and a police call center — but they can't reach everyone.

These services are still in early stages and face critical challenges in reaching the most vulnerable populations.

Rural Connectivity

Poor infrastructure and limited phone network coverage leave remote communities without access to existing helplines.

Girls with Disabilities

Current services are not designed to reach or support girls with disabilities — one of the most at-risk and underserved groups.

Early Stage Capacity

Both helplines operate with limited staff, limited hours, and limited language coverage — leaving most calls unanswered.

To protect survivors at scale, UNFPA needs a first responder that never sleeps — one that speaks Swahili, follows protocol, and connects every caller to a trained human counsellor.
